    The rules, as the facts file holds them

    What is set in the contract, and what is not guaranteed

    General practice, not a rule from the facts file.

    Typically set in the contract, if the required premiums are paid

    • The premium
    • The base death benefit
    • A table of cash values

    Not guaranteed

    • Dividends
    • The credited rate
    • Future dividend uses, such as additions not yet bought or a premium offset

    A dividend already added to a policy, for example as a paid-up addition, typically stays; a later lower dividend does not take it back.

    Why real dividends differ from an illustration

    General practice, not a rule from the facts file.

    An illustration shows what happens if today’s dividend scale never changes. It will change, so real values will differ.

    Illustrations often also show a lower scale for comparison; that is not a worst case.

    Small differences add up over decades.

    For a real policy, the insurer that issued it can provide an updated in-force illustration, and a qualified professional can explain it.

    Where to read the rules

    Words used here

    Par account
    The accounts a life insurer keeps for its participating policies, separate from those for its other policies.
    Dividend scale
    The formula that turns the account’s results into each policy’s dividend.
    Credited rate (dividend scale interest rate)
    The smoothed interest rate used inside that formula. It is not what a policy earns.
    Actual return
    What the account’s investments earned in a year at market value, with no smoothing.
    Smoothing
    Feeding good and bad years in gradually, so dividends move by small steps.
    Bond ladder
    Bonds bought so that some mature every year; the money is put back into new bonds at that year’s yield.
    Paid-up addition
    A small block of extra, fully paid insurance that a dividend can be applied to.
    Dividend class
    A group of similar policies that share experience and get the same dividend formula.
