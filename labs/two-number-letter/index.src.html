You asked when each of you could start the Canada Pension Plan and Old Age Security, and what difference the age makes. This letter sets out the rules and what they do to your numbers. It does not pick an age for either of you. That choice rests on things only the two of you know, and the questions at the end are a place to start.

What this letter does not weigh: health and how long either of you may live; income tax, and pension income splitting between you; the yearly indexing of both pensions; survivor benefits; how the recovery tax is withheld from one year's payments on the year before's income; and whether other savings can carry the years before a later start. Any of these can matter more than a crossing age.

What this ignores: inflation and indexing, tax, investment returns on money not yet spent, part-month starts, survivor and disability benefits, and the details of net income. The couple and their amounts are invented.

Questions worth bringing to a qualified professional who knows your full picture:

  • What do our own Service Canada estimates show for each of us?
  • If one of us starts later, which savings would cover the years in between, and what would that cost in tax?
  • How would each choice change the income of whichever of us lives longer?
  • Where would each of our incomes sit against the OAS threshold in each year, and can it be evened out?
  • How do these choices fit with when we draw on our RRSPs or RRIFs?
