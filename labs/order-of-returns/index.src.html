The order of returns

Same ten years, same average return, a different ending

Two made-up retirees get the same ten market years in a different order.

Synthetic sequence: not historical, not a forecast. No taxes, fees, pensions or government benefits. Education only.

Ruth and Sam are made up. Each retires with and takes out in the first year, more each year after to keep up with prices. Their first ten years are the same ten market years:

    The average of the ten is a year. Ruth gets them best first, Sam worst first. From year on, every year earns that same average, for both of them.

    No withdrawals

    Take the withdrawals away and the order stops mattering: each year multiplies the savings, and multiplying in a different order gives the same answer. Shuffle Sam's years as often as you like.

    With money coming out, a bad year early does more damage: the withdrawal is taken from savings that have just shrunk, and that money is not there when the good years come.

      Taking it further

      Questions worth asking

      Nothing here can say what anyone should do. It can help with the questions to take to a qualified professional who knows the whole picture:

      • Which of my costs are fixed, and which could bend in a bad year?
      • What would a poor first few years mean for my plan, and how would I want to respond?
      • How much do I want to hold outside the market, and what does that cost me?
      • Which steady income (workplace pensions, CPP, OAS) covers the costs that cannot bend?
      • If my savings are in a RRIF, how do the minimum withdrawals fit what I spend, and what happens to money I must take out but do not need?
      • How do taxes and fees change these numbers for me, and what if I live much longer than I expect?

      What this leaves out

      Taxes, fees, pensions and government benefits; real market history (these ten years are invented); spending that changes with age; prices rising by anything other than the assumed amount; and every year after the tenth being different from the average. It is a picture of one mechanism, not a plan.
